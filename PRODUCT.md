# The Quiet Line
<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform
web

## Stack
Delegated by the user: Three.js, native WebXR, Vite, plain JavaScript, with an original Blender-authored GLB carriage, five spirit passengers and little painted companions. Static output deployed through GitHub Actions/Pages. The worn-carriage extension follows the floor colony, quiet encounters and rear-wall repair; this work is maintained on `codex/newspaper-spirit` after the deployed compact-controls and quiet-train-audio release.

## Users
The owner targets Meta Quest 3 or 3S for later headset use; neither device has been physically validated. Desktop preview supports development and exploration without a headset.

## Product Purpose
An immersive, contemplative world inspired by the supplied Dreamy Ocean Sky Storyboard.png: dominant bright, deep blue sky, fewer deliberately placed painted clouds, expansive reflective ocean, and submerged railway.

## Capabilities and Constraints
User explicitly delegated sounds, movement, feel, assets, and design decisions, then authorized a public GitHub repository and GitHub Actions/Pages deployment. Preserve the reference. No account or backend required. Headset performance must be measured on hardware; do not claim verification without it.

The current journey begins at a fixed station on a raised grassy shore. Explicit boarding enters one carriage; left and right window seats, departure, pause/continue and return share the same actions on screen and the in-world VR menu. The train moves while the station and landscape stay fixed. Preserve gaze and real tracked head height, with no forced camera roll or bobbing. Free-water teleportation and the drifting-station concept are retired. The user retained station, boarding and seat actions; free walking is outside the current interaction design. Build and review this first carriage before adding more cars or journey content.

Controls should leave the scene open. Screen exploration keeps only Journey and Menu visible; secondary actions appear on request. The compact VR menu starts closed and opens only through an explicit grip action. Opening menus or adjusting time/sound must preserve the visitor's journey and gaze.

The user approved the original newspaper reader’s appearance, then requested three or four distinct additional spirits and reported that the page turned away from its reader. The local adult cast now has five: the corrected reader, a sleeper, a moth, a gardener and a keepsake holder. They share carriage movement, stay clear of both visitor seats, the aisle and doorway, and perform sparse independent gestures with still reduced-motion poses. The user subsequently requested 30% translucency: all five figures and their belongings use 70% opacity. This extension adds no interaction, dialogue, sound or free walking. Original Blender geometry reuses the reader atlas without new image generation; the original generated concept is material and silhouette guidance, not runtime evidence.

The user then approved a solid painted mouse and charcoal fuzzballs with white eyeballs, silent and mostly entertaining each other, with a couple scattered through the carriage. One mouse and its friend share a shallow wooden window perch above the right bench back; two more fuzzballs hop along separate empty left-bench cushions. Short travelling hops rise two to three of each creature’s own body height, with long rests between bursts. The mouse, three fuzzballs and perch are opaque; the five adult spirits retain 70% opacity. Preserve seats, aisle, doorway, visitor-controlled gaze and the still reduced-motion arrangement. This addition uses original editable Blender geometry and the unchanged reader atlas.

The quiet-encounter extension lets those creatures respond to sustained looking: a curious approach, brief mutual gaze, shy retreat and peek, then a return to their own lives. Only one creature seeks the traveller's attention at a time, with quiet rests between encounters. The mouse mostly watches outside, occasionally turning to its friend or the traveller; fuzzballs also sometimes watch the view. This uses camera/head direction rather than eye tracking and adds no sound, controls, gaze markers, instructions, assets or camera motion. New encounters are suppressed at the station, during transitions or open menus, and under reduced motion; an active encounter finishes safely after attention is lost.

The user requested five or six quick, curious floor creatures and accepted the live six-creature colony: “Yeah I'm looking at it. I think it looks great.” Nine fuzzballs and one mouse now inhabit the carriage. Floor creatures make independent decisions, scurry and investigate with uneven pauses, and use grounded leg strides instead of repeating hops. Keep their exact painted form, silence, opacity, reduced-motion stillness and bounded movement around passenger shoes and the traveller's feet. The three existing hoppers and mouse retain their quiet encounters. Shared geometry, materials and instancing limit the added rendering cost; hardware performance still requires measurement.

Sound starts only after interaction, defaults to 35% volume and retains mute/volume controls. Quiet filtered rolling noise and distinct, rounded, distance-driven track contacts accompany travel and settle to silence when paused. The user found the first mix too whoosh-like, then heard the bumps but reported an unpleasant low screech. The revised mix raises the paired bumps slightly, removes pitched train tones and softens water and wind inside the carriage. The latest tuning is retained for this release; actual Quest listening remains unverified.

## Brand Commitments
The supplied storyboard is the visual authority, refined by the user's explicit request for fewer clouds and dominant bright, deep blue sky. Later user references refine the clouds' feathery edges and pale-blue/mint washes, the white-and-teal dragon's appearance and more dynamic flight, and a small cottage island beside the railway. These extend the world while preserving the open sky, stable horizon and visitor-controlled camera. Project name and narrative are creative decisions delegated to the builder.

The train references establish a cream/maroon carriage, rounded roof, red bench upholstery, substantial dark window frames and hanging straps. Use original geometry and painted materials without copying reference-image pixels or model parts. The user rejected the current dragon's smooth procedural appearance; historical review approval is not user acceptance, and this carriage milestone does not replace that asset.

The user approved a worn, charming, Ghibli-like interior that feels used and
cared for, preferring upfront creation and baking work to expensive runtime
effects on Quest 3/3S. The local implementation has faded red cloth with two
stitched cushion repairs, polished timber, scuffed cream enamel, illustrated
route and seaside notices, carriage number 03, a static scarf and cloth parcel,
and soft vertex shading. These extend the existing world without new runtime
lights, shadow passes or animation. The independent finish review returned
**ship**; the user accepted the finished result: “I think we nailed it.” Physical headset
performance, stereo appearance and comfort remain unverified.

## Product Principles
Atmosphere first. Keep horizon stable. Movement is opt-in. Immersion must work without a desktop overlay. Assets ship with the application.
